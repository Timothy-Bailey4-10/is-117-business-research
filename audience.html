Audience decision

Who the guide is for first, and the reasons, with evidence separated from assumptions.

Proposed primary audience: college students, especially CS students, who want side income from software (Persona 1).
Secondary audience: new grad developers with a day job (Persona 2).

Status: PROPOSED. [TODO: Cs to agree or disagree in a GitHub issue; record the result in the Review log]

Evidence What the evidence shows

  • On the four competitor pages we captured, there was no path aimed specifically at students (S1–S4). F3
  • MicroConf Connect costs $49/month or $499/year and requires an application (S4). F2
  • MicroConf's Connect page names “aspiring founders … employed but seriously working toward launching” as members (S4), so Persona 2 is already partly served.

Limits: four pages from two sites is a small sample. Indie Hackers is not captured yet.

Assumption What we are assuming

  • Many students want side income from software.
  • Price and application barriers matter more to students than to employed developers.
  • Students would trust a guide made by other students.
  • Students have enough time (summers, breaks) to build something small.

Planned How we'll test these: short interviews or a survey with classmates. See Findings → decisions.

Why not choose Persona 2 first?

Analysis The evidence suggests employed developers already have a community built for them (MicroConf). Students looked less served on the pages we checked, and we have the most first-hand access to students for testing.
