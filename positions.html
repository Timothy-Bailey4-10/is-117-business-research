Two possible positions

A position is the one idea that makes our guide different. Here are two options, with the evidence and assumptions behind each.

Position A: The student's first-step guide

A free, beginner-friendly path from “I can code” to “my first paying customer,” written for students with limited time and money.

Why it might work

  • Evidence Assumption We saw no student-specific path on the competitor pages we captured (F3). Whether that gap exists elsewhere is an assumption.
  • Evidence Assumption MicroConf Connect costs $49/month and needs an application (F2). We assume a free option is attractive to students.
  • Assumption Our team are students, so we understand this audience.

Pros

  • Clear audience, easy to explain.
  • Fits our own experience.

Cons

  • Assumption Students have little money, so the guide itself may be hard to make profitable.
  • Assumption Lots of free general advice already exists (YouTube, Reddit) [TODO: check].

Position B: The honest-numbers guide

Every revenue or success claim is linked to its source and labeled “verified” or “self-reported,” with a focus on realistic, small outcomes instead of $1M stories.

Why it might work

  • Evidence Analysis Competitors lead with big revenue figures and unverifiable testimonials (F1, F4).
  • Assumption Cautious beginners would trust a source more if it showed its evidence.

Pros

  • Distinctive, trustworthy brand (calm reference guide).
  • Matches how we are already documenting this research.

Cons

  • Slower to produce: every claim needs checking.
  • Assumption Less exciting than big success stories, so it may get fewer clicks.

Current leaning: Analysis Combine them: Position A as the audience and Position B as the way we write. [TODO: Team decision; record it in the Review log]
