Research: one-person software businesses

This site documents the research stage of our IS117 group project. The future product is a guide that helps college students and new developers start small, profitable, one-person (solopreneur) software businesses: SaaS, apps, digital products and dev tools.

Status: version 0.1, first draft (2026-11-16). Every page exists, but many items are still marked [VERIFY: open the source] or [TODO: ...]. Nothing marked that way counts as evidence yet. See Change notes for what is still open.

Research question

What makes small software businesses run by one person profitable, and what do college students and new developers need to start one?

Who this is for

  • The future guide is for college students (especially computer science students) and new developers who want to start a small software business on the side.
  • This research site is for reviewers: our instructor and our teammate, who check that every claim traces back to a source.

Summary of findings so far

So far we have looked closely at two of the three competitors (Starter Story and MicroConf, captured 2026-11-16). The third, Indie Hackers, is still [TODO: capture].

  1. Evidence Both sites lead with social proof: project counts, revenue figures and testimonial walls. Finding F1
  2. Evidence MicroConf's private community costs $49/month or $499/year and requires an application. F2
  3. Evidence Assumption On the four pages we captured, we did not see a path aimed specifically at students. That this gap exists across the whole market is still an assumption. F3
  4. Evidence Analysis Starter Story shows revenue figures prominently. We did not see, on the pages captured, how those figures are verified. F4
  5. Analysis Both sites use all seven of Cialdini's persuasion principles. Their scarcity is about exclusivity, not countdown timers. F5
  6. [TODO: We have not yet found sources that answer the "what makes them profitable" half of the question. See open items.]

How to review this site

  1. Pick any finding on Findings → decisions.
  2. Open its source from the Sources page and check that the source really says that.
  3. Open a “Peer review” issue with what you checked, what is wrong and a suggestion.
  4. We log every issue and our decision in the Review & decision log.

How to read the labels

  • Evidence we saw it in a listed source
  • Analysis our interpretation of evidence
  • Assumption our belief, not yet tested
  • Simulated made up for planning, not real people
  • Planned a check we have not done yet
  • [VERIFY] must be checked against the source
  • [TODO] still to be filled in
